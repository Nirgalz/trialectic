## D-17 · Object / Subject / Focus restructuring (under development)
Date: November 14, 2026 | Status: **Validated decisions within an unfinished architectural restructuring**

The author adopts **Object** as the general type encompassing an Individual, Group, Technical Object, System, or Semantic Object. **Subject** denotes the Object placed at the center of an analysis. The main Subject is selected in the first **Stage** (a methodological operation); secondary Subjects may be analyzed in parallel, including within an Actualization Scenario. **Focus** denotes the current attention of the Method: initially the Subject, then a selected NEED **or** CAN, then Actualization, followed by reassessment of the Subject. An Object's boundaries are proposed through its existential and structural NEEDs, its component Objects, and its actionable CANs; detailed boundary and identity criteria remain to be formalized.

**CAN** denotes a Potentiality attributable to an Object; **NEED** denotes a necessity attributable to an Object. NEEDs and CANs are attributable properties that can establish relations within or between Objects, and their lists need not be exhaustive. Subject, Environment, and Semantics remain relevant perspectives on their determination without a presumption that all are equally weighted. The **Environment** is the contextualized set of Objects and relations liable to affect the Subject's NEEDs, CANs, or Actualizations. **Accessible Objects** are a subset with which the Subject can interact directly. A Semantic Pyramid is used for identifying NEEDs only when suitable for the Object; dependencies within a Semantic Object's Semantic Pyramid are conceptual and must not automatically be equated with empirical needs. Keep **Power** conceptually distinct from NEEDs and CANs.

**Scope caution:** The restructuring has not yet been integrated into the maintained alpha 0.3 reference. Operational definitions, relation types, Subject/Object identity, Focus procedures, and remaining consistency questions are still open. Related tasks: T-01, T-02, T-03, T-05, T-30.

## D-18 · Approved NEED definition and satisfaction states
Date: November 14, 2026 | Status: **Validated**

Author-approved French definition (preserved verbatim):

> **NEED :** nécessité attribuable à un Objet, dont la satisfaction est requise pour l'existence, le maintien ou la réalisation d'un état déterminé de cet Objet. Un NEED peut être existentiel, structurel ou les deux. Sa satisfaction peut résulter d'un état existant ou impliquer l'Actualisation d'un ou plusieurs CANs

## D-19 · NEED–CAN condition validation and circular dependency
Date: November 14, 2026 | Status: **Validated principle; operational criteria open**

A NEED–CAN relation can contribute to validating an Actualization Condition, **but only after checking that the CAN is effectively mobilizable**. Matching a NEED with a CAN must not be treated as proof that a NEED is actually satisfied or that an Actualization succeeds. A NEED relevant to an Object's Actualization may belong to another Object in its Environment.

For a **circular NEED–CAN dependency between two Objects**, the Method should **seek a third Object that can satisfy a NEED of either of the first two Objects** and thereby potentially break the cycle. Finding such an Object does not by itself establish mobilizability, actual satisfaction, or resolution; those checks remain necessary. Cases where no suitable third Object is found remain open. Related tasks: T-02, T-16, T-17, T-30.

## D-25 · Object Definition, permanent identity and constitutive necessity
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated principles; operational criteria OPEN**

The author validated a unique, permanent **ID** for every Object and a versioned, evolving **Definition** (the name remains provisional). The Definition is **not only a list of constituent Objects**: it consists of constituent Object references **plus associated properties and relations**, including relevant NEEDs, CANs, their interactions, partial knowledge and uncertainty. Ordinary modifications of the Definition do not require a new Object ID.

A Subject is an Object at the center of the attention of the **Method or analyst**. Objects are initially identified in the focal Subject's Environment, prioritizing the Subject's existential and structural constituent Objects. The Definition can expand contextually and recursively, including reassessment when an Object is Actualized. A proposed test of constituent necessity is: **without that Object, can the Subject still exist?** Distinguish **conceptual existence** from **functional existence** when applying the test. A Semantic Pyramid can help assess constitutive necessity **when relevant**; conceptual necessity is not automatically an empirical necessity.

Partially known NEEDs and CANs may be represented in a Definition with **qualitative uncertainty** (missing/insufficient information, evidence of non-necessity) and/or **quantitative/statistical uncertainty** where supportable. The distinctions among no evidence, incomplete evidence and evidence of absence must be preserved. Group/System CANs must be distinguished from constituent-Object CANs and may involve coordinated Actualizations of multiple constituents.

**OPEN:** Operational thresholds and category-specific existence/necessity tests, representation and calibration of uncertainties, how a focal Subject is initially chosen, and detailed Object-identity tests. Related tasks: T-30, T-01, T-05, T-15, T-03.

## D-26 · Parent/Children branching, shared constituents and historical Definitions
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated principles and branching rules; implementation OPEN**

Each Object has a **Parent** reference (`null` or the Object from which it branches), a **Temporary Children** list, and an **Actualised Children** list. Parent is a **lineage** relation and is distinct from the constituent-Object relations of the Definition.

Contradictory Actualizations affecting one Object create distinct child Objects with different IDs and Definitions while retaining the original Object as the reference parent. This applies to **simulated alternative Scenarios** and to **effective Actualizations**. The approved contradiction criterion is mutually incompatible states of the same Object property **within the same reference context**; mere change over time need not be contradictory. A simulated branch is **temporary**; at effective Actualization it is moved from the parent's Temporary Children to Actualised Children **without changing its ID**. Simulations and their side effects remain isolated from effective states until Actualization. An effective change propagates across current Definitions referring to shared constituent Objects.

Historical Definition versions must retain their past contents and the relevant **versions** of constituent Objects. Thus modifying a current shared Object must not retroactively alter historical Definitions. All effects on relevant NEEDs, CANs and Conditions require reassessment where applicable. The original Object and initial Definition remain traceable. Keeping a simulated Object does not establish an independently existing physical entity.

**OPEN:** Precise branch-ID allocation and namespaces, simultaneous contradictory effective observations, conflict handling across simulations with shared constituents, physical instance semantics, and snapshot/transaction algorithms. Related tasks: T-30, T-05, T-03, T-16, T-18, T-24, T-21.

## D-27 · CAN actionability, optional conceptual-Object representation and uncertain Power
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated terminology and distinctions; precise conditions OPEN**

A CAN is selected and described with context-relevant granularity; **a CAN may be modeled as a conceptual Object with its own NEEDs only when useful**, not universally. The author questioned whether a distinct category of **"attributable CAN"** remains useful: its formal retirement is **OPEN**, and earlier constitutive-contribution criteria are preserved as research material without forcing an additional present classification.

The preferred term is **CAN actionable**: its relevant **conditions of action are fulfilled**. A separately classified **"CAN mobilizable"** is no longer needed. The author retained the distinction between **CAN actionability for a specified relationship/contribution** and **the complete sufficient Conditions of Power** for the considered Actualization; the exact scope and logical interface remain OPEN, especially when both refer to identical conditions. Earlier D-20/D-21 wording remains in the historical log and is not silently rewritten.

NEED–CAN correspondence, NEED satisfaction, CAN actionability, full Power and completed Actualization remain distinct analytical checks. When several CANs correspond to a NEED, or several NEEDs to a CAN, create and evaluate relevant Scenarios; evaluate effects when A's NEED conditions B's Actualization. An already satisfied NEED can be examined for **maintenance, possible transformations, or no required intervention** rather than forcing an Actualization.

With uncertainty, the author allows **statistical evaluation of Power**, while distinguishing `P(Power positive | available information)` from `P(successful Actualization | available information)`. Do not impose ungrounded numerical estimates. An effective Actualization updates the **Definitions of affected Objects**.

**OPEN:** Operational actionability/Power distinction, statistical estimators/calibration, whether 'attributable CAN' remains a status, and CAN-as-Object criteria where representation is helpful. Related tasks: T-02, T-01, T-16, T-18, T-22, T-30.

## D-28 · Default Advanced Mode, multi-Scenario exploration and suspension of Simple Mode
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated operational objective and scope**

The author adopted **Advanced Mode as the default functioning** of the Trialectic Method. For complex problems, the Method seeks to **generate and present multiple relevant Resolution Scenarios** with anticipated effects on the **initial/focal Subject** and Objects in its **Environment**, and to make their trade-offs and limits interpretable. Its goal is **not to choose one universally best solution**.

The author explicitly validated these distinct evaluation dimensions and safeguards:
- **Feasibility**, **complexity**, **cost/resources**, **temporality**, and **expected effectiveness** are evaluated separately, rather than conflated.
- Distinguish (a) uncertainty / probability that the **conditions for feasibility** are met, (b) **probability of successful Actualization**, and (c) probabilities of **individual anticipated consequences**. Use statistical probabilities **only when justifiable**; otherwise describe qualitative uncertainty or degree of possibility without inventing percentages.
- Evaluate relevant favorable and unfavorable consequences on other Objects, including important indirect effects, without masking serious harm in a single aggregate score.
- Maintain the **same methodological rigor** even if presentation or interaction is simplified. The analysis need not select a winning Scenario.
- Separate **Scenario generation**, **Scenario evaluation**, and **optional selection**. If selection is ever requested, a recommendation is **conditional on explicitly stated criteria**, not an objective universal optimum.

The author decided to **set aside Simple Mode for now**. Its usefulness will be **reconsidered after the Method is stabilized**. Simple Mode is therefore **not a current operating mode, default, implementation commitment, or required experimental arm**. Earlier documentation considering both modes is historical; it is superseded by this scope decision, not erased.

**OPEN:** Quantitative calibration and empirical evaluation of feasibility/success/consequence uncertainty, measuring Scenario diversity and coverage, selecting an appropriate number of alternatives, standardized cost/complexity measures, and criteria for optional selection when requested. The approved operational goal is not an empirical proof of effectiveness. Related tasks: T-03, T-04, T-16, T-17, T-19, T-20, T-21, T-26, T-27.

## D-29 · Recursive NEED–CAN comparison and candidate Scenario construction
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated procedural principles; implementation and empirical tests OPEN**

The author approved a concrete **NEED-focused** route for generating multiple Resolution Scenarios:

1. Compare each relevant **non-satisfied NEED** against contextually relevant **CANs of the focal Subject and Objects in its Environment**.
2. Identify **alternative CANs**, which may independently offer candidate approaches to a NEED, and **complementary CANs**, which may need to be combined, sequenced or coordinated. Combine relevant NEED–CAN links into candidate Scenarios, without presuming any one correspondence proves sufficiency.
3. Inspect the **conditions of action** and any relevant supporting **NEEDs** for each participating CAN. Where these are not met, recursively search for other Objects, NEEDs and CANs that could satisfy or enable the missing conditions, including pathways through circular dependencies.
4. **Include a CAN in a candidate Scenario even if it is not currently actionable**, provided the **missing conditions are explicitly documented**. Such inclusion is conditional: it does not imply positive Power, satisfaction, guaranteed success or an effective Actualization.
5. **Evaluate the resulting Scenarios separately** for actionability, Power, NEED satisfaction, dependencies, consequences on the Subject and Environment Objects, feasibility, uncertainty and other contextually relevant criteria. Reassess hypothetical versions of affected Definitions while respecting isolated simulations, recursive relevance and cost limits.

The author expressly approved distinguishing **(a) a NEED–CAN correspondence**, **(b) complementary relations supporting a candidate pathway**, and **(c) an evaluated Scenario**. A correspondence is not an Actualization; an evaluated Scenario is not necessarily viable. **Scenario generation, evaluation, and optional selection remain distinct operations** in default Advanced Mode.

This decision does **not** override the already validated **CAN-focused** Stage-2 option, nor require an Actualization for a NEED already satisfied by an existing state.

**OPEN:** Exact matching and evidential criteria for correspondence, determining independent or joint sufficiency of combinations, path deduplication and diversity, circular-dependency handling, termination rules, representation of missing conditions, and independent reproducibility and controlled comparative testing. Related tasks: T-16, T-02, T-03, T-17, T-21, T-24, T-27, T-30.

## D-30 · Three distinct NEED–CAN pathway cases for Scenario generation
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated conceptual distinction; operational tests OPEN**

The author explicitly validated distinguishing **three cases** in the construction of NEED-focused Resolution Scenarios:

1. **Single-CAN satisfaction pathway:** one CAN may suffice to satisfy the focal NEED **under specified conditions**. This is a candidate sufficiency relationship; identifying the CAN does not by itself prove actionability, positive Power, effective Actualization or observed NEED satisfaction.
2. **Joint-CAN satisfaction pathway:** multiple CANs must contribute together (possibly sequentially, in parallel or under conditions) to satisfy the focal NEED; no single contributor is assumed sufficient by itself.
3. **Enabling-CAN pathway:** one CAN does **not directly satisfy** the focal NEED, but helps meet a missing condition of action of another CAN, potentially making that other CAN **actionable**. The relevant missing conditions must be identified and may be examined recursively.

**[FORMALIZATION, not separately adopted as an exclusive taxonomy]** These cases classify **roles or relations within an identified pathway**, not three fixed, mutually exclusive kinds of CAN; a CAN can fulfill different roles in different Scenarios, and a compound Scenario can combine joint and enabling dependencies. The shorthand names above are working English labels, not new definitive bilingual terminology.

**OPEN:** Criteria and evidence for sufficiency of a single CAN, joint necessity and minimal combinations, causality and indirect enabling effects; treatment of partially known and conditionally actionable CANs; overlap between roles, branching costs and duplicate pathways; tests on held-out cases. This updates the T-16 generation protocol without declaring it complete or scientifically validated. Related tasks: T-16, T-02, T-24, T-03, T-17.

## D-31 · Correspondence, contribution, conditional sufficiency and relative necessity of NEED–CAN relations
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated conceptual criteria and verification framework; operational proof and implementation OPEN**

The author explicitly validated the distinction among **four context-dependent NEED–CAN relations**:
1. **Correspondence:** a CAN bears a relevant, explainable relationship to a NEED. Mere similarity in terminology is not adequate causal evidence.
2. **Contribution:** Actualization of a CAN can favorably modify conditions for satisfying the NEED, under the stated context. Contribution by itself implies neither necessity nor sufficiency.
3. **Conditional sufficiency:** **A CAN, or an identified set of CANs, is sufficient for satisfying a NEED when its Actualization makes it possible to reach the required state of that NEED in an explicitly specified context and under explicitly specified conditions.** This does not establish present actionability, positive Power, successful implementation, or observed NEED satisfaction.
4. **Scenario-relative necessity:** **A CAN is necessary for a particular Scenario when its absence prevents that same Scenario from satisfying the NEED concerned.** The test must not silently substitute a different CAN or another Scenario while removing the candidate.

The author validated **two levels of necessity**: necessity **within a specified Scenario** and necessity **across the currently identified relevant Scenarios**. Neither establishes absolute necessity across all possible but unidentified pathways. This analytical necessity is distinct from the conceptual or functional existential necessity of an Object.

The author validated a **five-question verification framework** for each assessed NEED–CAN relation: (i) justify correspondence and mechanism; (ii) compare plausible contribution **with and without** the CAN's envisaged Actualization; (iii) verify conditional sufficiency for the required state under a stated period/context/conditions; (iv) examine necessity by removal **within the same Scenario**; and (v) assess actionability and document/recurse on missing conditions. Sufficiency and necessity concern satisfaction of a NEED, while actionability concerns conditions for undertaking an Actualization; keep those tests separate.

The author also validated a working representation using **OR** for alternative pathways, **AND** for jointly necessary CANs, and **enabling dependencies** where one CAN helps satisfy another CAN's missing action condition. These relations do not alone represent time-dependent, non-monotone or destructive effects; those still require reassessment of relevant Object Definitions and conditions.

Finally, the author validated **four epistemic qualifications** of a relation:
- **Established within the considered model**: follows from explicit assumptions of the model; *not* necessarily empirically established.
- **Plausible**: explanatory mechanism identified but evidence insufficient for confirmation.
- **Refuted in the considered context**: available evidence contradicts the relation in that context.
- **Indeterminate**: information is insufficient to conclude.

These labels concern the **analyst's knowledge**, not intrinsic NEED or CAN states. Quantitative statistical probabilities may supplement them only when evidence justifies such estimates. An assessed relation must not be presented as empirically established solely because it follows from a model.

**OPEN:** Evidence and causal-inference criteria for contribution; counterfactual removal tests in changing environments; precise sufficiency and joint necessity tests; alternative-path completeness; statistical calibration; minimal sets and path equivalence; time and non-monotone dynamics; independent reproducibility. This decision elaborates D-29 and D-30 without closing T-16 or claiming scientific validation. Related tasks: T-16, T-02, T-03, T-24, T-17, T-21.

## D-32 · Temporal satisfaction requirements and recursive discovery of unknown NEED–CAN relations
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated principles; shared temporal data format PROPOSED, not validated**

The author validated that the **required state of a NEED can include an explicit temporal requirement** specifying when and for how long the state must be satisfied. The Method must evaluate **conditional sufficiency** relative to that temporal requirement, including cases where uninterrupted satisfaction is required over part or all of a Scenario. A final state satisfying a NEED does **not** establish that it remained satisfied throughout the Scenario. The time-related properties of a NEED's Definition and the state/satisfaction observed or simulated in particular Scenarios must not be conflated. A simulator assessing continuity must not falsely claim continuous compliance when violations might occur between its observation points.

The author approved two complementary operations:
1. **Exploitation of known relationships:** search documented relevant NEEDs, CANs, actionable conditions and dependencies.
2. **Exploration of missing relationships:** when known candidates do not suffice, recursively broaden relevant Object Definitions and the contextual Environment to identify additional possibly existing Objects, CANs or initially unknown relations. Any proposed relation must be identified as **hypothetical** and subjected to the D-31 checks of correspondence, contribution, conditional sufficiency, Scenario-relative necessity and actionability. Semantic associations alone are not empirical causal evidence. Apply recursive relevance and complexity limits.

The author also validated distinguishing three information states: **(a) no suitable Scenario currently identified**, **(b) a hypothetical or unverified candidate**, and **(c) assessed infeasibility/impossibility of examined pathways under explicitly specified conditions**. Neither incomplete inventories nor the failure to identify a solution prove that NEED satisfaction is universally impossible. Discovery of a previously unidentified CAN must remain distinct from **development/emergence of a new CAN** under Plasticity.

**[PROPOSED DATA MODEL — NOT VALIDATED]** The author requested a reusable temporal data format attachable to both a NEED and a CAN. A common structured field for temporal requirements and estimated action/effect timing has been proposed for discussion. Its JSON field names, constraint modes (instant, continuous, deadline, periodic, cumulative), interval conventions, evidence metadata and evaluation schema have **not** yet been explicitly approved. The approved conceptual temporal rule does **not** imply approval of these implementation choices.

**OPEN:** Shared temporal schema; semantics and granularity of continuity/interval requirements; temporal effects and within-action events; discovering unknown relations reproducibly; preventing unbounded exploration; evaluating evidence and contextual impossibility; comparative validation. No task is closed or scientific effectiveness established. Related tasks: T-01, T-03, T-16, T-18, T-21, T-24, T-30.

## D-33 · Validated common temporal data representation for NEEDs and CANs
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated format, five modes and separation of Scenario evaluations; detailed operational semantics OPEN**

The author explicitly validated the proposed common time-related data format, the five modalities, and the strict separation of required constraints from Scenario-specific assessed states.

**[VALIDATED]** A NEED or CAN may use the same `temporal` structure. Its `requirements` capture time constraints (e.g. when a NEED must be satisfied, when a CAN must begin), and its `estimates` capture estimated CAN execution time, delay of effects and persistence of effects. A `time_zone` identifier supports the local civil-time context. Reusing the structure does **not** equate a NEED's required satisfaction state with a CAN's action conditions or outcome.

**[VALIDATED]** Five reusable constraint modes are supported by the working data representation:
- `point`: specified instant (`at`).
- `continuous`: required satisfaction throughout an interval (`window`).
- `deadline`: compliance no later than a stated deadline (`deadline`).
- `recurring`: repeated requirement (`recurrence`).
- `cumulative`: minimum accumulated duration (`min_duration`) within a `window`.

Multiple constraints may apply to the same NEED or CAN; the **precise logical combination rules remain OPEN**, rather than assuming unapproved semantics for conflicting, overlapping or nested constraints.

**[VALIDATED representation conventions]** Use ISO 8601 offset-aware timestamps; durations use ISO 8601 (such as `PT30M`); time intervals are **inclusive at the start and exclusive at the end**; lack of a recorded or known duration must be distinguished from a known **zero** duration. Estimated durations can be expressed as ranges (`min`/`max`). A NEED's temporal requirements are recorded with its versioned Definition, rather than being silently rewritten when the NEED becomes temporarily satisfied or unsatisfied.

**[VALIDATED separate Scenario evaluation]** Actual or simulated time-indexed satisfaction status and violations of a NEED, and evaluation evidence, belong to a **Scenario-specific assessment separate from the NEED/CAN `temporal` metadata**. Continuous satisfaction cannot be inferred from final-state success alone, nor asserted with inadequate temporal observations. The examples and illustrative JSON structure in `reference/trialectic-method.md` are accepted as the working format, **not** yet a complete and independently validated machine-enforced JSON Schema.

**[OPEN]** Precise semantics of constraints used together; recurring scheduling and exceptions; cumulative duration aggregation; relative-event temporal references; time-zone daylight-saving changes, event ordering and interval edges; provenance and uncertainty of estimates; temporal resolution within actions; schema validation and multi-Object simulator testing. Related tasks: T-03, T-16, T-18, T-24.

## D-34 · Combining temporal constraints, authorized exceptions and three-valued evaluation
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated temporal combination rules and illustrated JSON; detailed schema/algorithm tests OPEN**

The author explicitly validated rules A–E and the proposed JSON representation (section 6 of the discussion), building upon the common NEED/CAN `temporal` format approved by D-33.

**A. Default conjunction (VALIDATED).** When a NEED or CAN has multiple applicable temporal requirements, **all** must be satisfied unless explicitly authorized alternative satisfaction conditions are represented. Satisfying a cumulative-duration requirement cannot compensate for an interruption that violates a separately mandated continuous requirement.

**B. Explicit alternatives and composition (VALIDATED).** Use `all` for requirements that must all be satisfied and `any` when satisfying at least one requirement is enough. The optional `composition` field contains an `operator` and a `requirements` array referencing requirement IDs; without `composition`, apply `all` by default to the documented requirements. This does not make an incomplete list exhaustive. Complex logical groupings are conceptually allowed, but exact nested object serialization, reference validation, and precedence remain OPEN. Alternative requirements within one NEED/CAN must not be confused with distinct Resolution Scenarios.

**C. Authorized exceptions (VALIDATED).** Exceptions to requirements must be **explicitly allowed**, **time-scoped**, **justified**, and retained in the **versioned history**. They must not be inferred from an observed interruption, silently retroactively legalized, or allowed to bypass unrelated applicable constraints. Detailed exception object structure, authority, amendments and overlapping-rule precedence remain OPEN.

**D. Contradictions (VALIDATED).** Identify requirements that demand mutually incompatible states for the **same Object/property/context and overlapping time**. Distinguish:
- compatible constraints (can be assessed together);
- mutually incompatible requirements (a **Definition/constraint inconsistency** to be investigated);
- compatibility indeterminate owing to missing information.
A contradictory Definition is distinct from an otherwise coherent NEED being **unsatisfied in a Scenario**. Contradiction/consistency diagnostics do not establish a fourth intrinsic NEED-satisfaction state.

**E. Three-valued aggregation (VALIDATED).**
- `all`: **satisfied** if every requirement is satisfied; **unsatisfied** if any is unsatisfied; otherwise **indeterminate** when at least one is indeterminate.
- `any`: **satisfied** if any requirement is satisfied; **unsatisfied** if every requirement is unsatisfied; otherwise **indeterminate** when at least one is indeterminate.
- Structural contradictions are flagged separately and must not silently be absorbed into the three-valued satisfaction result.
- When computing cumulative satisfaction time, **overlapping satisfied intervals must be counted only once**.

**Section 6, approved JSON example (VALIDATED).** The following `temporal` structure adds an optional `composition` field to D-33; this is a **working example**, not a complete JSON Schema:

## D-35 · Distinct effective NEED satisfaction and temporal compliance evaluations

The author explicitly approved **all three proposals A–C** following the experimental D-34 time engine:

## D-36 · NEED and CAN as origin-anchored relations connecting one or more Objects
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated data-structure and participant-cardinality clarification; operational and logical semantics OPEN**

The author reformulated the representation of a NEED and a CAN: rather than only a property of an Object or necessarily a separately instantiated conceptual Object, **each NEED/CAN is represented as a typed relation between Objects**, with the following directly specified relational record fields:
- `ID` — identifier of the NEED/CAN relational record;
- `Type` — `NEED` or `CAN`;
- `NEEDs` — nullable list of **Objects** (Object references) that can be used to refine granularity and specify the parent NEED;
- `CANs` — nullable list of **Objects** (Object references) used to refine the parent NEED or CAN;
- `OriginObject` — the Object from which the particular NEED or CAN originates.

The author further clarified that the 'second Object' in the relation is **identified directly in the `NEEDs` or `CANs` list(s)**. When only one Object occurs across the lists, the relation has a single other Object participant; when multiple Objects occur, the relation has **multiple other Object participants**. **A separate mandatory `TargetObject` field is therefore unnecessary.** Both lists may be present; their detailed semantics remain to be formalized. Describing the resulting data structure as an origin-anchored multi-party relation (directed hyperrelation) is a **technical interpretation**, not a separately approved ontological thesis.

The referenced entries in the lists are **Objects**, not automatically sub-records of type NEED/CAN or Objects whose global type becomes NEED or CAN. The `Type` field identifies the **relation record**. The Object-oriented and versioned Definitions of D-26/T-30 remain relevant, but prior representations in which NEEDs and CANs appeared merely as Object properties or a CAN was only optionally a conceptual Object must be **reconciled with this newly validated default relational structure**. Prior conceptual NEED (necessity), CAN (Potentiality), actionability, Power, temporal and Scenario-satisfaction distinctions have not been replaced.

**No unapproved inference:** The presence of several referenced Objects does not establish joint necessity (`AND`), alternative sufficiency (`OR`), contribution, actual NEED satisfaction, CAN actionability or a causal relation without further analysis. The relation record may support progressively more granular descriptions and recursive exploration, but the rules for composing those refinements are not yet fixed.

**OPEN:** Exact meaning and combination of `NEEDs` and `CANs` when both contain Object references; whether all list members are requirements, alternatives or other relations; meaning of null and empty lists, including when no participant is currently named; whether duplicates may occur; recursive nesting and parent/child relation identity; versioning of relational records, preservation of origin and target references under branch Actualization; interaction with shared Objects and temporal metadata; need for additional attributes. Related tasks: T-30, T-01, T-02, T-03, T-16, T-24.

## D-37 · Structured NEED/CAN relation: origin type, referenced-Object roles and composition
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated relation dimensions, category catalogue, basic JSON representation and per-link design principle; detailed per-link schema and empirical checks OPEN**

Following D-36, the author explicitly validated all five parts of the proposal for a **structured `relation` field** on the NEED/CAN origin-anchored relational record. A single exclusive relation label is insufficient: the data model must distinguish (i) **nature of the relation relative to `OriginObject`**, (ii) **roles toward individual Objects referenced in `NEEDs` or `CANs`**, and (iii) **logical grouping/composition** of those references. `OriginObject` remains the relevant origin field; do not confuse it with the pre-existing Object lineage `Parent`.

**1. Validated relation nature relative to origin.** Contextually applicable classifications may coexist:
- **NEED**: **existential** (necessary to existence in an explicitly stated sense), **structural** (necessary to a defined organization/configuration), **functional** (necessary to functioning or maintenance of a specified state).
- **CAN**: **contributive** (potential beneficial contribution), **enabling** (can help meet another CAN's action condition), **inhibitory** (may impede actions or their conditions), **transformative** (may change Object constitution, NEEDs or CANs).
These are descriptions of a specific analyzed relationship, not unconditional global properties. Necessity and sufficiency, in the distinct senses tested in D-31, are not fixed independent relation categories.

**2. Validated catalogue of contextual relationships between multiple referenced Objects/contributions.**
- **Alternative**: different possible paths to the stated outcome, without assuming each is independently sufficient.
- **Complementary**: participating or jointly contributing Objects; not an automatic proof of individual necessity.
- **Substitutive**: one Object can replace another for a specified role; stronger and more specific than unspecified alternatives, but not necessarily perfect interchangeability.
- **Sequential**: contributions are ordered in the relevant pathway.
- **Synergistic**: a combined contribution or effect cannot be represented as a simple sum of isolated effects, and requires investigation.
- **Antagonistic**: interactions conflict, inhibit or limit one another.
- **Reciprocal**: mutual dependencies or contributions.
Multiple labels may apply in specific contexts, and their mechanism must be assessed rather than asserted from similarity alone.

**3. Validated additional relation qualifiers.** Where relevant record **conditionality**, **temporality**, **direct or indirect causality**, and **epistemic evidence/status**. Retain D-31 distinctions (established **within a model**, plausible, refuted within the studied context, indeterminate), without confusing qualitative labels with statistically founded likelihood. Keep D-33–D-35 time requirements, exceptions, effective satisfaction, compliance and Scenario evaluations separate where applicable.

**4. Validated initial `relation` JSON format.** The author accepted the extension to the D-36 record, with `relation.nature` (multiple compatible values) and `relation.composition` containing Object groups and `all`/`any` operators:

```json
{
  "ID": "R-001",
  "Type": "NEED",
  "OriginObject": "O-001",
  "NEEDs": ["O-002", "O-003"],
  "CANs": ["O-004", "O-005"],
  "relation": {
    "nature": ["existential", "structural"],
    "composition": [
      {"objects": ["O-002", "O-003"], "operator": "all"},
      {"objects": ["O-004", "O-005"], "operator": "any"}
    ]
  }
}
```

These are **asserted groupings**, not in themselves proof of satisfaction, causality, Power or absolute necessity. The logical interaction between **different composition groups** still requires specification. The `relation.composition` for **Object-reference links** is separate from the previously validated D-34 `temporal.composition` of **temporal requirements**.

**5. Validated design recommendation: qualified links.** The `relation` field must be capable of describing each linked Object's **own role**, because one CAN could be enabling for another CAN, contributive for a NEED, and inhibitory for a different Object at the same time. The `nature` field for the origin relationship must not silently propagate to every reference. **The precise machine-readable substructure for per-link attributes is NOT yet validated**; names like `links` or `qualifiers` would remain proposals until specified and approved.

**OPEN:** per-link record shape and IDs; multi-group Boolean logic and precedence; semantics when one Object is referenced in both lists; existential/structural/functional classification operational tests; context-sensitivity, relation versioning/branching, negative effects and conflicts, empirical evidence standards and independent reproduction. This decision elaborates D-36 and D-31 without claiming that every listed relation is empirically established. Related tasks: T-30, T-01, T-02, T-03, T-16, T-24.

## D-38 · Individually qualified Object links in NEED/CAN relations
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Validated proposed link structure and safeguards; full graph semantics and implementation OPEN**

The author explicitly validated **all five proposals A–E** for the NEED/CAN relational data model, elaborating D-36 and D-37:

**A. Qualified-link list (VALIDATED).** Add `relation.links` to the origin-anchored NEED/CAN record. Each qualified link describes a relationship to a referenced Object that appears in one of the parent record's `NEEDs` or `CANs` lists. This is distinct from global `relation.nature` (toward `OriginObject`) and from `relation.composition` (logical groupings of multiple Objects).

**B. Working field structure (VALIDATED).** Each explicitly recorded link has **three required fields**:
- `id`: identifier of the link record;
- `object_id`: identifier of the linked Object;
- `source_list`: `NEEDs` or `CANs`, specifying which list of the parent relation contains the Object.

The optional fields are:
- `roles`: one or several contextual roles, e.g. `contributive`, `enabling`, `inhibitory`; omission can represent an unresolved qualification;
- `conditions`: relevant applicability/action conditions;
- `mechanism`: explanation or hypothesized mechanism;
- `causality`: direct, indirect or indeterminate characterization where justifiable;
- `evidence`: epistemic status and supporting justification, consistent with D-31.

**C. Link-to-link effects (VALIDATED).** Permit optional `affects` containing IDs of **other links** that the current link may enable, inhibit or otherwise modify. This supports relationships among relationships, not merely Objects linked independently. An `affects` assertion alone does not prove which exact condition was affected, causality, feasibility, necessity or sufficiency. Recursive chains, cycles, cross-record addressing and version-specific references remain to be designed and tested.

**D. Referential coherence (VALIDATED).** Every link's `object_id` must be present in the enclosing parent relation's inventory named by `source_list`: if `source_list` is `NEEDs`, the Object must appear in `NEEDs`; likewise for `CANs`. Introducing an unlisted Object requires **explicit expansion of the inventory** or an appropriate additional relation. This rule does **not** imply that every inventoried Object already has fully known link qualifications, or that the inventory is exhaustive. Link IDs should distinguish the links that are individually referred to; the full scope and persistence of uniqueness remain OPEN.

**E. Causal and logical safeguard (VALIDATED).** Labels such as `contributive`, `enabling`, and `inhibitory`, including `affects` edges and `all`/`any` groupings, are **classifications and proposed analysis claims**, not proof of the NEED's satisfaction, causal contribution, a CAN's actionability, Power, conditional sufficiency, Scenario-relative necessity or completed Actualization. Preserve D-31 checks and evidential qualifications.

## D-39 · Semantics of link-to-link effects and dynamically evaluated circular dependencies
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Five analytical and design principles A–E VALIDATED; illustrative `effect_details` structure and full algorithmic semantics OPEN**

The author explicitly approved **all five proposals A–E** following D-38. The Method now separates the existence of a link-to-link relationship from its possible triggering conditions and its observed or simulated effects, and distinguishes a structural dependency cycle from its operational consequences in a Scenario.

**A. Qualify what a link affects (VALIDATED).** The existing optional `relation.links[].affects` field continues to reference the IDs of other links. The influence must be capable of **separate, explicit qualification**, as appropriate, by which aspect it changes: **action conditions** of a CAN, **conditions of NEED satisfaction**, **availability/emergence/modification/loss of a Potentiality/CAN**, or **contextual validity/applicability of the relationship itself**. Effects may be favorable, unfavorable or transformative. These are **affected aspects**, not necessarily four new exclusive relation types. Preserve the D-31 requirement for mechanism, conditions and supporting evidence.

**B. Structure versus effective blockage (VALIDATED).** An oriented graph of `affects` relationships can contain circular dependencies without implying a logical contradiction, impossibility or functional blockage. Distinguish: (i) a **blocking cycle** in the examined context when all necessary prior conditions are missing and no actionable known initiation path exists, (ii) an **already initiated/sustaining cycle** with relevant initially satisfied conditions, (iii) a **third-party-initiable cycle** if an external CAN can supply a missing initial condition, and (iv) a **potentially antagonistic cycle** whose enabling/inhibitory effects can interact dynamically. These are **Scenario-specific evaluations**, not immutable global cycle categories. Absence of a known pathway is not proof of absolute impossibility.

**C. Recursive exploration for potential cycle initiation (VALIDATED).** When an identified cycle appears to prevent an Actualization, recursively explore relevant CANs and Objects within or beyond the cycle that could satisfy a missing action or satisfaction condition, change the Environment or alter relevant dependencies. A third CAN can sometimes **initiate** a self-maintaining loop without eliminating the loop's underlying structure. Do not claim resolution merely because a candidate CAN has been found: test its own actionability, causal mechanism, timing, downstream effects and uncertainty in the relevant Scenario, in accordance with D-31–D-35 and existing recursive relevance limits.

**D. Distinguish relation, trigger and effect (VALIDATED).** Represent separately:
1. a link and its potential relationship to another link (`affects`);
2. the **trigger/event and conditions** under which the relationship might produce an effect;
3. the **actual or simulated state transition/effects**, recorded in the relevant Scenario, with chronology and evidence, **not** silently written back as historical facts into the versioned Object Definition.
The author approved the **principle** and the potential addition of optional detailed metadata such as `effect_details` as an **initial operational illustration to test**, **not a finalized, machine-enforced schema**.

    {
      "target_link_id": "L-A",

Here `can_id` refers to a hypothetical CAN relational record, `object_id` to an Object, and `target_link_id` to another link. The reference scopes, types, provenance and time logic remain to be specified.

**E. Detect cycles structurally; evaluate their dynamics (VALIDATED).** Construct a directed dependency/effect graph from the relevant `affects` edges and detect circular structures (e.g. using **strongly connected components**). Then **evaluate separately**, for each contextual Scenario, initial state, actions that are actually feasible, effect triggers, temporal sequence, non-monotone inhibition, alternative pathways, evolving Object Definitions and evidential gaps. This is a hypothetical mechanism to check under stated technical assumptions, not proof that every such system behaves in this manner.

## D-40 · Identifiable conditions, time-indexed evaluations and Actualization-triggered state changes
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Five conceptual and data-design principles A–E VALIDATED; example JSON field layout and formal schema PROVISIONAL / OPEN**

Following the D-39 cycle experiment, the author explicitly **validated proposals A–E** on conditions. The Method shall separate **the Definition of a condition**, **its evaluation in a particular Scenario at a time or over a time interval**, and **events/Actualizations that change the relevant Object state or Definition**. A condition is an **identifiable, contextual requirement** relevant to a CAN's actionability, a NEED's satisfaction, or a relationship's applicability. It need **not** itself be an independent Object.

**A. Conditions as identifiable requirements (VALIDATED).** A condition may be specified in or associated with the appropriate NEED/CAN relational record or an individually qualified link, with a stable identifier, target/purpose, a predicate over the state of one or more Objects and, where relevant, composition and temporal constraints. Keep distinct the **requirement** and its **referenced Object(s)**; stating a predicate is not proof that the predicate holds. **B. Definition versus evaluated state (VALIDATED).** Do **not** store an unqualified current truth/status as the immutable property of the condition's Definition. Derive an evaluated satisfaction state using available **observed or simulated Object properties** and context in the relevant **Scenario**, at a specified time or interval, then record that derived assessment with traceability. Historical evaluations remain pinned to their corresponding Object/relationship/condition Definition versions; Scenario results do not silently rewrite Definitions.

**C. Three-state evaluation and composition (VALIDATED).** Condition evaluations support `satisfied`, `unsatisfied` and `indeterminate`, with evidence/uncertainty recorded separately. Even if the known required action conditions are satisfied, the method must not silently infer complete Power, sufficiency for a NEED or effective Actualization from the limited scope of that evaluation.

**D. Actualizations modify states or Definitions; then conditions are reevaluated (VALIDATED).** An effective or simulated Actualization changes the state of one or more Objects, and may exceptionally entail an explicitly versioned **change of Definition** (including the Definition of a condition). Record the event, changes, chronology, cause/uncertainty and assessment in the relevant Scenario, preserving D-33/34 temporal conventions, D-35 satisfaction-versus-compliance and the D-39 link/trigger/effect distinction. Never silently overwrite historical Definitions.

**E. Condition identity scoped to its owning relation and versioned references (VALIDATED).** Each condition has a stable identifier **in the scope of its owning NEED/CAN relational record**: a pair such as `R-A / C-external` identifies the owning relation and its condition. Use the associated Definition version (or equivalent version reference) for historical evaluations and Scenario comparisons. This avoids requiring a new globally autonomous condition Object, while permitting `affects` / experimental `effect_details` to target a condition unambiguously **when ownership and version are specified**. Further cross-record/branch link and condition reference formats remain OPEN.

## D-41 · Completeness of action pathways and inventories, with nested conditional composition
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **Five conceptual principles A–E VALIDATED; a single recursive expression schema is PROPOSED for review and testing, NOT yet approved**

The author explicitly validated **all five proposals A–E** distinguishing **knowledge of the necessary conditions within an identified pathway** from **knowledge of all contextually relevant pathways** to Actualize a CAN. This decision extends D-40 without replacing D-31 necessity/sufficiency distinctions or D-33–D-35 temporal requirements.

**A. Two levels of completeness (VALIDATED):** (1) **internal pathway completeness**, determining whether all relevant conditions needed for the **specified pathway and context** have been identified; (2) **pathway-inventory completeness**, determining whether all pathways relevant to the CAN **within the specified analytical scope** have been identified. Completeness at either level must not be transferred to the CAN as an unconditional global property.

**B. Context, version and justification (VALIDATED):** All completeness claims require explicit analytical **scope**, Definition/version context, assumptions, and supporting **evidence**. `complete` means **claimed complete for the specified model/scope**, not empirically proved exhaustive. Keep `incomplete` and `indeterminate` distinct when recording the knowledge or status of inventory coverage.

**C. Nested logical compositions (VALIDATED):** The Method must represent explicitly grouped `all`/`any` compositions among required conditions **within a pathway** and alternative identified pathways **within an inventory**, with nesting where necessary. Syntactically valid AND/OR groups and correspondence/links are **not proof** of actionability, Scenario-relative necessity, conditional sufficiency, Actualization or NEED satisfaction.

**D. Separate evaluation of each pathway and the inventory (VALIDATED):** Apply the D-40 three-valued statuses `satisfied`, `unsatisfied`, `indeterminate` separately to **identified pathways** and **the set of identified pathways**, taking account of the completeness declaration at **each relevant level**. Under an inventory known to be complete (for the selected scope), any **confirmed satisfied** pathway suffices for a satisfied inventory; all confirmed unsatisfied pathways produce an unsatisfied inventory; otherwise indeterminate. With an **incomplete or undetermined inventory**, a genuinely confirmed satisfied pathway can still establish a satisfied **modeled** route; all known pathways failing means **indeterminate** global actionability in the open inventory, not unsatisfied. Within an **incomplete/undetermined pathway**, failure of a known necessary condition can make that particular identified path unsatisfied, whereas apparent satisfaction of all known conditions is **indeterminate** until omitted required conditions have been excluded for the studied scope. Unknown data on an individual condition is not a missing Definition, and an undiscovered pathway is not an unknown condition value. Always document evidence and known limits. Never claim complete CAN Power or successful Actualization solely from an evaluated pathway.

**E. Pathway identity in the data model (VALIDATED):** Introduce identifiable **Actualization pathways** with a composition of conditions, a pathway-specific completeness assessment and supporting evidence; retain a distinct completeness assessment of the collection of identified pathways. The exact JSON serialization, persistence, reference identity and source of evidence are still OPEN.

**[PROPOSED ONLY — NOT VALIDATED] Single recursive logical-expression representation.** To avoid three unrelated expression schemas (`condition_composition`, `pathway_composition`, etc.), the assistant proposes using one recursive syntax for **Boolean composition** and named/typed references: `{"op":"all"|"any","items":[expression,...]}` or `{"ref":{"kind":"condition","id":"C-..."}}`. Pathway/inventory metadata would attach to appropriately identified expression groups, including `id`, `scope`, `coverage`, `evidence` and `kind` as applicable. An expression group could contain nested groups or references to other named pathways, but explicit reference resolution and cycle handling must be developed. Coverage is **metadata on the relevant group**, not a third logical operator; eval must account for as-yet unknown members according to the D-41 rules. For a group with `all`, a confirmed unsatisfied known child remains unsatisfied even with unknown additional conjuncts, but otherwise an incomplete group cannot be confirmed satisfied. For a group with `any`, a confirmed satisfied known child remains satisfied even with unknown additional alternatives, but otherwise an incomplete group cannot be confirmed unsatisfied. These candidate operational rules are an extrapolation from validated D-41 principles, not a finalized evaluator.

**OPEN:** Review and approval of exact recursive syntax; whether expression tree nodes and named pathways require stable IDs, typed cross-relation references and definition-version pins; empty/nested groups, reference cycles, mixed operator precedence, completeness evidence and confidence, overlap with D-37 `relation.composition` and D-34 `temporal.composition`, conditions that involve time or emergence, and executable independent tests. ## D-42 · One recursive logical-expression representation, reusable versioned references and safe cycle handling
Date: November 15, 2026 | Status: **VALIDATED principles and three rules A–C; exact JSON fields and evaluation algorithm remain experimental**

The author explicitly validated (1) **the principle of a single recursive logical-expression structure**, and (2) **all three proposed rules A–C** for that structure. This elaborates D-41. The approved structural idea is to use a **uniform recursive grouping mechanism** rather than unrelated syntaxes for conjunction/disjunction of pathway conditions and for alternative named pathways, while retaining the distinct meanings/scopes of condition composition, Object-reference composition (D-37) and temporal requirement composition (D-34).

**A. Reusable, explicitly versioned references (VALIDATED):** One group may reference a previously defined named pathway instead of copying its expression. Reuse must preserve **an explicit identifier and Definition-version reference**; the precise compound reference schema (owning relation, branch, namespace and version) and cross-record resolution rules remain to be formally specified.

**B. Empty groups (VALIDATED):** An `all`/`any` group with **zero children** must **not** silently receive the conventional Boolean identities `true` for empty AND or `false` for empty OR. Such a group is a **missing/incomplete or invalid Definition**, to be flagged diagnostically. The exact distinction between structural invalidity and a valid "no identified pathways" state in an open inventory is OPEN; no automatic satisfaction/unsatisfaction or statement of impossible Actualization is authorized.

**C. Cyclic references (VALIDATED):** An identified pathway may reference another that references it. Such cyclic structures can be **represented and detected**, but must **not** be evaluated through naïve unlimited recursive descent. Detect cycles explicitly and route them to D-39's **Scenario-dependent dynamic dependency analysis**. Circularity is not by itself evidence of an actual blocking cycle, impossibility, positive Power or successful Actualization. If an experimental evaluator derives a conservative Boolean result for part of a cyclic group, this remains distinct from a proof of the temporal sequence or feasibility of involved CAN Actualizations.

**[PROPOSED working serialization — NOT YET APPROVED AS FINAL SCHEMA]** Represent a group as `{"op":"all"|"any","items":[expression,...]}` and a typed leaf as `{"ref":{"kind":"condition"|"pathway","relation_id":"...","id":"...","version":"..."}}`. Named expressions may carry `id`, `kind`, `scope`, `coverage`, `version` and evidence metadata. The fields `relation_id` and `version` on leaves illustrate how to implement rule A; the exact field names, ownership and serialization have **not** separately been approved. A group may contain leaves or other groups and represent nested conjunctions/disjunctions. D-41 context-scoped pathway and inventory completeness must remain separate metadata; it is not a new logical operator.

**[TEST REQUEST]** The author explicitly requested tests of these rules, especially typed versioned reuse, empty `all`/`any` handling, cyclic reference detection and contextual behavior. Synthetic execution results may be documented as preliminary engineering evidence, but are not approval of the provisional JSON Schema, independent empirical validation or closure of any TODO.

**OPEN:** final JSON Schema, identities/versions across branches and owning relations, treatment of no-known-path inventory without an invalid empty group, positive recursive Boolean fixed-point semantics versus dynamic causal reachability, truth-status/evidence provenance, time-indexed and non-monotone effects, integrity of references, cyclic graphs crossing records and independently validated tests. Related tasks: T-02, T-03, T-16, T-18, T-24, T-30.
