# Trialectic Method
## A different way to understand complex situations

### Understand before trying to solve

Why do certain difficulties persist despite the solutions we implement?

Why can two people examine the same situation and reach different conclusions?

Why can a solution that works under certain circumstances become ineffective, or even problematic, when the context changes?

These questions have something in common: they concern situations in which multiple elements interact and influence one another.

We often tend to isolate problems, look for a principal cause, and favor a single solution.

This approach can be useful, but it becomes insufficient when difficulties result from numerous relationships and dependencies.

**The Trialectic Method proposes an analytical framework for understanding these relationships, identifying opportunities for change, and exploring several Resolution Scenarios.**

Its purpose is not to provide an automatic answer, but to support a more structured and thorough understanding of the situations examined.

## A method applicable to different fields

The Trialectic Method can be used to investigate a wide variety of situations.

Someone wishing to change careers may want to understand their possibilities, constraints, and the consequences of different choices.

An association facing difficulties may analyze its resources, operations, and relationships with other organizations.

A local authority may explore several public policies by examining their effects on residents, institutions, and the services concerned.

A philosophical disagreement may be examined by clarifying its underlying concepts, representations, and lines of reasoning.

These situations differ, but they can be approached using shared methodological principles.

## Understanding elements and their relationships

The Method uses the term **Object** for anything that can be examined: an individual, a group, a technical object, a system, or a semantic object, such as a concept.

The Object at the center of an analysis is called the **Subject**.

The Subject is not examined independently of the context relevant to the question at hand.

Its **Environment** includes other Objects and relationships that may influence the situation.

For example, understanding a person's professional opportunities may require examining their knowledge, health, financial resources, social network, training providers, and the labor market.

The analysis seeks to identify important relationships rather than describe every element exhaustively.

It can also shift the focus to another Subject to investigate a relationship or dependency more deeply.

## Identifying what is necessary and what is possible

Two notions occupy a central place in the Method.

### NEEDs: necessities

A **NEED** is a necessity attributable to an Object for a specified state of that Object to exist, be maintained, or be realized.

An organization may need certain resources to maintain its activities. A technical system may require an electricity supply to perform a particular function.

A necessity may already be satisfied, remain unsatisfied, or have a satisfaction status that cannot yet be determined.

A NEED does not always imply that new action is required: existing conditions may already satisfy it over time.

### CANs: potentialities

A **CAN** is a Potentiality of an Object: a possibility that can be attributed to it.

A person may have the Potentiality to acquire a skill. An association may have the Potentiality to organize an activity. A tool may contribute to completing a task.

A Potentiality is not necessarily immediately available for implementation.

Its Actualization may depend on conditions that have not yet been met, including interventions by other Objects.

The Method therefore examines possible correspondences between the NEEDs of some Objects and the CANs of others, without assuming that a correspondence is already a solution.

## Distinguishing possibility from effective action

The Method distinguishes three complementary concepts.

**Potency** denotes the set of Potentialities attributable to an Object at a particular time.

**Power** determines whether sufficient conditions are met to undertake the Actualization of a particular Potentiality.

**Actualization** means the effective implementation of that Potentiality and the resulting effects.

These distinctions matter.

A person may possess a Potentiality without currently having the conditions needed to implement it.

Conversely, meeting the conditions required to undertake an action does not guarantee that the action will achieve its intended outcome.

The effects of an Actualization may also transform Potentialities, necessities, and relationships between the Objects concerned.

## Exploring several scenarios

When the Method is used to address a problem, it does not necessarily seek a single solution.

It examines several relevant scenarios.

Some may draw on already accessible Potentialities. Others may require new resources, cooperation among several Objects, or a prior change in certain conditions.

Each scenario is examined along several dimensions:

- Its feasibility and related uncertainties.
- Its complexity and implementation difficulties.
- Its cost and the resources required.
- Its timing and the delays involved.
- Its expected effectiveness.
- Its favorable or unfavorable consequences for the Subject and other Objects concerned.

An advantageous short-term solution may create long-term difficulties. A transformation that benefits one group may impose constraints on another.

The Method encourages making these consequences visible rather than hiding them behind an overall assessment.

**Comparing scenarios does not necessarily mean declaring a winner.** Any eventual selection depends on explicitly stated objectives and decision criteria.

## Clarifying meanings

Understanding a situation sometimes requires examining the meanings we assign to things.

Two people may use the same words without giving them exactly the same meaning.

They may also appear to share an objective while holding different representations of what it entails.

The **Semantics** perspective allows the analysis to examine concepts, interpretations, beliefs, and the relationships among them.

It can help clarify a problem, identify contradictions, or reveal previously overlooked alternatives.

This perspective is used when it contributes to the analysis. It is not meant to constitute a mandatory independent stage or to receive the same weight as the other perspectives in every case.

## An understanding that evolves

Systems are not static.

A transformation can change the conditions that made an action possible, bring new Potentialities to light, or create new dependencies.

The Trialectic Method is therefore **recursive**: it allows an analysis to be revisited in light of observed changes or newly acquired knowledge.

It also distinguishes anticipated consequences from those actually observed. A consequence does not become a fact merely because it appears in a scenario.

Exploration should not continue indefinitely: it proceeds as long as the additional relationships and consequences examined remain sufficiently relevant to the analytical purpose, while giving particular attention to potentially major risks.

## A method for developing understanding

The Trialectic Method can be used individually, in collective discussion, or with the assistance of artificial intelligence.

In the latter case, AI may help make assumptions explicit, identify relationships, propose scenarios, and examine their consequences. Its proposals must nevertheless remain open to verification and discussion.

The Method does not claim to eliminate uncertainty, resolve all conflicts, or objectively determine the best decisions.

It aims to make analyses more explicit, assumptions easier to examine, and alternatives more intelligible.

**Its ambition is to foster an understanding of complex situations that helps reveal new possibilities, anticipate consequences, and revise conclusions when necessary.**

---

*The Trialectic Method is an analytical framework under development. Its principles have undergone conceptual formalization, but its effectiveness and comparative value have not yet been scientifically validated.*
