# RD-01 — Analytical relevance, cognitive effort and exploration depth

**Date:** 2026-11-20 · **Status:** APPROVED RESEARCH DIRECTION; procedures/hypotheses OPEN.
**Links:** [research index](../README.md), [RD-03](../interaction/progressive-analyst-experience.md), [D-89 relevance](../../DECISIONS.md), [existing synthetic relevance pilot](../tria-dev-relevance-focus-pilot-2026-11-17.md).

## Question

How should the Method and trIA choose, justify, revisit and stop analytical operations without exhausting the analyst or neglecting an important affected Subject, unresolved contradiction or consequential adverse possibility?

## Theoretical starting points (not equivalent theories)

1. **Sperber & Wilson, *Relevance: Communication and Cognition* (1986/1995):** contextual cognitive effects relative to processing effort, and expectations raised by communication. It does **not** supply a universal numerical equivalence of effort and effect. What merits examining and what merits communicating are distinguishable questions.
2. **H. A. Simon, bounded rationality and satisficing:** limited time, information and processing capacity can justify a goal-relative stopping criterion, not a false claim of exhaustive knowledge.
3. **Decision theory, Value of Information:** further investigation is useful insofar as its possible findings can materially improve an assessment or action, when weighed against its costs. It is not identical to the total educational value of an analysis.
4. **Russell & Wefald, rational metareasoning; Lieder & Griffiths, resource-rational cognition:** choose between *computations or reasoning operations* given expected contribution and cost. Any numerical utility model requires calibrated assumptions; default to qualitative justifications.
5. **Pirolli & Card, Information Foraging Theory:** intelligible indications of expected value influence where people spend search effort.
6. **Sweller, cognitive-load theory; Bjork, desirable difficulties:** distinguish extraneous complexity from productive intellectual work.
7. **Eccles/Wigfield and Deci/Ryan, motivational approaches:** perceived value, expected success, autonomy and competence can help form hypotheses about engagement, not universal guarantees.

These are research leads, not automatic conceptual imports or claims that the Trialectic Method has been scientifically validated.

## Existing Method constraints

- **D-89:** evaluate local Focus *and* the broader problem, affected Subjects, significant contradictions, indirect effects and severe risks. A narrow Focus cannot act as an exclusion filter.
- **Recursive relevance:** explore effects while plausible, important and consequential enough; retain rare-but-serious risks.
- **D-93:** examine validity and robustness proportionately to stakes and uncertainty.
- **D-85:** Understand → Explore → Decide are recursive phases; a particular analysis can stop after Understand or Explore.

## Working hypothesis (not a new mandatory Stage)

An analytical operation merits further effort when the expected contribution to the declared analytical purpose, understanding, alternatives, justification or learning is sufficiently important relative to its costs, while respecting global relevance and safety/epistemic safeguards.

Separate:
- **anticipated relevance** before work from **observed usefulness** after it;
- **analytical contribution** from **communicative clarity** and **learning value**;
- **user cost** from **AI or external verification cost**;
- **immediate contribution** from delayed, indirect or high-stakes potential;
- **documented absence of evidence** from evidence of absence.

## Candidate operation-selection questions

For an optional next inquiry, briefly ask:
1. What is the precise unresolved question, and which Subjects/aims may be affected?
2. What could change if we answered it (diagnosis, CAN/NEED attribution, alternatives, robustness, understanding)?
3. What is required of the Analyst, AI and external sources?
4. What risk remains if we defer, and which important omissions might exist outside the current Focus?
5. Is this an appropriately scoped pause, further check or return to an earlier phase?

No universal score, mandatory form, automatic stopping algorithm or exhaustivity claim is approved.

## Concrete tests

- Compare explicit eight-step explanation, brief adaptive explanation and pedagogical guidance on the **same** frozen simple, complex and consequential cases.
- Assess accurate understanding, justified decisions, missed affected Subjects, traceability, analyst effort, perceived usefulness and later independent problem-solving; include cases where additional work has no benefit.
- For severe uncertain consequences, compare a simplistic cost/benefit stop against the D-89 safeguard and document unresolved risk.

**Failure conditions:** over-narrow Focus, unjustified elimination of low-likelihood severe risks, self-confirming relevance judgments, fabricated numerical accuracy, or simplified steps that hide key uncertainty.
