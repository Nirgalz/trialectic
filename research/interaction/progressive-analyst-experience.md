# RD-03 — Progressive, pedagogical and immediately useful trIA interaction

**Date:** 2026-11-20 · **Status:** guiding principles APPROVED BY AUTHOR (D-98); UI mechanisms and cognitive outcomes OPEN.
**Links:** [research index](../README.md), [RD-01](../methodology/analytical-relevance-and-effort.md), [RD-02](../representation/semantic-objects-and-sets.md), [RD-04](human-and-technical-terminology.md).

## Approved guiding principles (original wording)

> Ne pas demander davantage d'effort à l'utilisateur avant de lui avoir rendu intelligible ce que cet effort pourrait lui apporter.

> Ne pas supprimer les efforts qui contribuent effectivement à développer sa compréhension et son autonomie.

These principles are compatible **only if** productive difficulty is distinguished from avoidable administrative or terminological burden.

## Hypotheses

- The user can better decide whether to deepen when trIA explains **the possible value of the operation**, not merely its procedural name.
- The **immediate result of each action** (a clarified question, new distinction, inspected link, located contradiction, useful alternative or better calibrated uncertainty) should be visible when possible.
- A progressive representation can remain analytically rigorous even when its default view is simple and its detailed qualifications are disclosed on request.
- Asking the analyst to reason through a meaningful alternative or objection can improve learning more than auto-completing every step. These are **testable hypotheses**, not universal psychological facts.

## Candidate interaction contract — not yet a normative requirement

For a proposed optional operation, trIA may present, succinctly:
- **Question / why:** what remains unresolved;
- **Potential gain:** what could change after doing it;
- **Effort:** what the analyst must provide, what trIA can do, what external checking requires;
- **Limit of skipping:** what remains uncertain, and whether severe risks require attention;
- **Immediate result:** what became clearer, which new possibilities or doubts appeared.

Example, avoid technical-first prompts:
- Less accessible: `Identify the Conditions of Actualization of the focal CAN.`
- More accessible: `We can examine what is stopping this possibility from being carried out. That may distinguish an actual obstacle from an assumption.`

This is a communication example, **not** a mandatory simplification of the analytical model or proof that identified obstacles are actual.

## Design boundaries

- Analyst controls expansion, stopping, return and interpretative choices. AI suggestions do not silently become selections or established claims.
- Preserve the D-89 broader-question/affected-Subjects safeguard: local convenience cannot suppress serious omissions.
- Do not claim a short analysis exhausts the question; allow a meaningful conclusion after Understand or Explore, consistent with D-85.
- **No automatic reward score or universal cost/effect ratio.** Important benefits may be delayed; an unattractive but essential verification may still be justified.
- Human-facing labels should default to comprehensible language while machine records retain stable identities and constraints; see RD-04.
- The prototype's simple/detailed **map presentation** is **not** the Method's deferred Simple Mode.

## Test protocol (proposed)

Compare identical initial analyses with (A) technical-step prompts, (B) expected-gain explanations, (C) expected-gain explanations plus analyst reasoning prompts. Record perceived value, task completion, error/omission rate, handling of uncertainty, time, willingness to continue and later ability to explain a new problem independently. Include novices and experienced users when possible, and record negative cases. No efficacy claim before such evaluation.
