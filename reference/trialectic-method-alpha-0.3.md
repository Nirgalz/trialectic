# Trialectic Method — Reference alpha 0.3 (November 14, 2026)

Version: **alpha 0.3** · Date: **November 14, 2026** · Status: **working reference**, not scientifically validated.
Consolidation sources: reference 1.0 (November 12, 2026), reference alpha 0.2 (November 13, 2026), decision log, and clarifications developed during task T-01. Previous versions are preserved.

## Reading convention
- **VALIDATED**: an explicitly adopted conceptual decision.
- **FORMALIZATION**: a methodological elaboration consistent with decisions, without word-for-word validation.
- **OPEN**: a criterion, hypothesis, or procedure that still requires testing.
- Examples illustrate rules; they do not constitute empirical validation.

# 1. Definition, scope, and purpose
The **Trialectic Method** is an interdisciplinary, contextual, systemic, and recursive analytical framework intended to understand Subjects' Potentialities, the conditions for their Actualization, the resulting transformations, and the relationships between Subjects and Environments. It mobilizes the perspectives of **Subject**, **Environment**, and **Semantics** according to their relevance, without treating them as three universal causes or imposing symmetrical weight on them. **[VALIDATED]**

The usual Subjects of analysis are individuals, groups, technical objects, and systems. Semantic objects are within the scope of analysis; their autonomous status as Subjects remains experimental. **[VALIDATED / OPEN]**

A possible purpose is to make contradictions visible, seek alternatives, and examine how to increase individuals' Potency, Power, and Actualizations without diminishing those of others. This normative orientation must be distinguished from descriptive findings. **[VALIDATED / FORMALIZATION]**

# 2. Conceptual glossary

## 2.1 Subject, Situated Subject, Environment, Semantics
- **Subject [VALIDATED]**: an entity selected as the unit of analysis. Autonomous action is not required for a Potentiality to be attributed to it.
- **Situated Subject [VALIDATED]**: a Subject considered within its Environment; contextualization selects internal properties, external elements, and relevant relationships according to the Focal Potentiality.
- **Environment [VALIDATED]**: elements and relationships external to the Subject that are relevant to the analysis.
- **Semantics [VALIDATED]**: a perspective concerned with meanings, representations, interpretations, and contradictions of meaning; it can clarify purposes and reveal alternatives. Its importance depends on the case.

## 2.2 Potency and Potentialities
- **Potency [VALIDATED]**: the set of a Subject's Potentialities at time T; it evolves.
- **Potentiality [VALIDATED]**: an aptitude or possibility of Actualization attributable to a Subject, including when implementation depends on other Subjects.
- **Focal Potentiality [VALIDATED]**: the Potentiality selected as the main object of analysis; it guides contextualization, the evaluation of Power, and the search for Scenarios.
- **Plasticity [VALIDATED]**: a Subject's aptitude for its Potentialities to emerge, change, or disappear; it is distinct from change actually observed.
- **Intrinsicality [VALIDATED]**: the property of being inherent in the Subject's category or its particular constitution. A categorical intrinsic Potentiality belongs to the definition of a category, without necessarily being manifested immutably by every concrete Subject. A specific intrinsic Potentiality may change along with the Subject's constitution.
- **Specificity [VALIDATED]**: a property particularly characteristic of the Subject, without necessarily being shared by its category.
- **Origin [VALIDATED]**: a dimension distinct from intrinsicality; a Potentiality may be initially present, acquired, or emergent. An acquired Potentiality may be intrinsic to the present constitution.

## 2.3 Conditions, Power, Actualization
- **Conditions of Actualization [VALIDATED]**: conditions allowing the implementation of a Potentiality to be **undertaken**, without guaranteeing the intended outcome.
- **Condition status [VALIDATED]**: whether each condition is satisfied at time T; this can change without a transformation of the Potentiality.
- **Alternative pathways [VALIDATED]**: distinct options satisfying the same condition, or distinct sets of conditions allowing an Actualization to be undertaken.
- **Power [VALIDATED]**: a property assessed for each Potentiality at time T; **positive** if at least one sufficient pathway is satisfied, **negative** otherwise. Missing information may yield an **indeterminate assessment**, which represents the analyst's uncertainty rather than a third ontological state of Power.
- **Actualization [VALIDATED]**: the effective implementation of a Potentiality and the effects that follow, including feedback on Potency, Power, Plasticity, and Environment.
- **Focal Actualization [VALIDATED]**: Actualization of the Focal Potentiality.
- **Improving Actualization [VALIDATED]**: an Actualization envisaged or undertaken to improve the Power or prospects of success of another Potentiality; it may remain relevant even when focal Power is already positive.

# 3. Constitutive attribution of Potentialities

## 3.1 Central principle
**[VALIDATED]** “A Potentiality is attributable to a Subject when one or more identifiable properties of its constitution ground a determinate possibility of Actualization. Such attribution requires neither autonomous action, nor current satisfaction of the Conditions of Actualization, nor exclusive attribution to that Subject. The criteria for establishing this foundation vary according to the type of Subject studied.”

The constitutive contribution must be **relevant** to the Actualization considered; the mere abstract possibility of a future transformation does not suffice to attribute today all Potentialities that could eventually be acquired. **[VALIDATED; operationalization OPEN]**

## 3.2 Present Potentialities, learning, and transformations
**[VALIDATED]** Distinguish a Potentiality currently attributable from one that might emerge after a transformation of the Subject's constitution.

Example: a person who cannot play the piano may possess the Potentiality to **learn** to play it, depending on their constitution and the relevant conditions. Actualizing learning may bring about the Potentiality to **play** the piano. These Potentialities must not be conflated. Suitable learning pathways may differ; no particular physical prerequisite should be presumed universal.

The Potentiality for learning may itself be focal or may feature in a Scenario supporting another Focal Potentiality. **[VALIDATED]**

**[FORMALIZATION]** Distinguish:
- **Discovery**: identifying a Potentiality already present but previously unknown.
- **Development**: transforming the constitution or Potency, potentially causing a Potentiality to emerge or change.
- **Redefinition**: changing the Potentiality selected as focal.
These operations may be combined.

## 3.3 Individuals, collectives, and third parties
**[VALIDATED]** A collective Potentiality is attributed to the **group as Subject**, not automatically to each of its members. Individual Potentialities may contribute to collective Actualization without becoming identical to the group's Potentiality.

When a Focal Actualization depends on another Subject's Actualization (an individual, group, technical object, or system), this dependency must be made explicit among the Conditions of Power and may be organized within a Resolution Scenario. **[VALIDATED]**

## 3.4 Three provisional tests for task T-01
### [OPEN — tests retained, not yet adopted as definitive criteria]
- **A — Present foundation**: identify constitutive properties at T without presupposing a necessary future transformation.
- **B — Discriminating contribution**: verify that the foundation is sufficiently specific to the Actualization to avoid indiscriminately attributing every Potentiality to every Subject.
- **C — Individualized attribution**: distinguish the collective Subject's Potentiality from individual contributions, without automatic transfer of attribution.

**Open questions:** threshold for relevance of the contribution; distinction between a constitutive property and a Condition of Power; granularity of Actualization; evidence and uncertainty of attribution; reproducibility between analysts.

# 4. Focal Potentiality and negative Power
**[VALIDATED — terminological correction]** A Focal Potentiality may have **negative Power** and remain unactualized. The Method must be able to examine missing conditions, propose redefining the Focal Potentiality, consider developing or acquiring other Potentialities, and complete the contextualization of the Situated Subject, notably by identifying previously omitted elements of the Environment.

This situation must not be described as “negative Potency”: Potency denotes a set of Potentialities. There is no need to introduce a new formal category of “targeted Potentiality” or a positive/negative attribution status. **[VALIDATED]**

**[FORMALIZATION]** The analysis distinguishes two diagnoses: (a) the Potentiality is attributable but its Power is negative; (b) current attribution of the Potentiality has not been established. In the second case, first examine the constitutive foundation, the discovery of existing Potentialities, or their possible development, rather than immediately assigning Power to a supposedly acquired Potentiality.

# 5. Resolution Scenarios and recursive planning

## 5.1 Scenarios
**[VALIDATED]** A **Resolution Scenario** organizes one or more Improving Actualizations, sequentially, in parallel, or conditionally, with a view to improving Power or the prospects of success of the Focal Actualization. It may involve multiple Subjects, include dependencies, and provide for reassessment.

Positive focal Power does not rule out seeking improvements: the ability to undertake an action does not guarantee success. A candidate Improving Actualization whose Power is negative may be retained as a future option if the missing conditions are specified. **[VALIDATED]**

## 5.2 Evaluation and comparison
**[VALIDATED]** Compare Scenarios along four contextual dimensions:
- **Mobilization**: resources, constraints, dependencies, feasibility, and difficulty, with feasibility and difficulty remaining distinct.
- **Temporality**: delays, sequences, urgency, and time horizons.
- **Expected Effectiveness**: envisaged improvement in Power and prospects of success.
- **Broader Consequences**: effects on other Potentialities, Subjects, and Environments.

Also consider internal coherence, robustness, adaptability, complementarities, synergies, competition for resources, and major risks. Weights are not universal; an aggregate score must not conceal adverse effects that cannot be offset. **[VALIDATED]**

**[FORMALIZATION]** An illustrative score may take the form Σ(weight × rating) / Σ(weights), using conventional ratings from 0 to 5. It is neither a probability nor an objective truth. Calibration, double counting, and ranking stability remain **[OPEN]**.

## 5.3 Recursive loop
### [VALIDATED / FORMALIZATION]
1. Define the Situated Subject, Focal Potentiality, and purposes of the analysis.
2. Verify the constitutive foundation and characterize relevant Potentialities.
3. Identify the Conditions of Power, their status, alternative pathways, and dependencies.
4. Assess Power and missing information.
5. Explore Improving Actualizations and construct Scenarios.
6. Anticipate and compare effects, risks, and interactions.
7. Undertake selected Actualizations when their Power allows it.
8. Observe effects; distinguish what was anticipated, what was observed, and what can be causally attributed.
9. Reassess Potency, Power, Conditions, and Environment; revise the Scenario or Focal Potentiality if necessary.

A simulation is not an effective Actualization. The loop may also reveal a pre-existing but previously unidentified Potentiality. **[VALIDATED]**

# 6. Effects, temporality, and complexity management
**[VALIDATED]** Distinguish two independent dimensions: **direct / indirect** (causal mediation) and **immediate / delayed** (temporality). A direct effect may be delayed; an indirect effect may be immediate.

Distinguish **probability** (likelihood of occurrence), **degree of possibility** (non-probabilistic plausibility), **magnitude** (importance of the change), and **value** (favorable or unfavorable appraisal under an explicit criterion). Also distinguish anticipated, observed, and causally attributed effects.

The **principle of recursive relevance [VALIDATED]** calls for continued exploration of indirect effects while their possibility, magnitude, and relevance to Subjects and objectives justify it; do not overlook a rare but potentially major risk. The **principle of complexity management [VALIDATED]** permits documented simplification or suspension of analysis that is too uncertain or costly, without presenting this limit as an absence of effects.

# 7. Semantics and transformations of meaning
- **Semantic Landscape [VALIDATED]**: the structured and evolving set of an individual's knowledge, beliefs, representations, values, interpretations, and relationships of Meaning; its reconstruction remains partial.
- **Semantic Object [VALIDATED as a landscape component]**: a unit of meaning identifiable through a **Meaning**, one or more **Expressions**, and a **Conceptual Network** contributing to that Meaning. Different Expressions or Networks may carry similar Meanings.
- **Semantic Pyramid [VALIDATED as a principle]**: hierarchical organization of a Conceptual Network's dependencies relative to a Meaning or focal concept; networks need not be tree-shaped.
- **Constitutive concept [VALIDATED as a distinction]**: removing it makes the Meaning in question impossible. **Significant concept [VALIDATED as a distinction]**: removing it substantially changes the Meaning without making it disappear. Non-circular identification of these concepts still requires testing.
- **Semantic Plasticity [VALIDATED]**: the aptitude to transform the Semantic Landscape, without assuming that every transformation changes Potency.

**[OPEN]** Semantic equivalence, identity of Meaning, joint/alternative/circular/indirect dependencies, hierarchical and structural importance, and reconstruction without analyst projection.

# 8. Minimal formalization
**[FORMALIZATION]** Let S be the Situated Subject, T the time studied, P(S,T) its Potency, p an attributable Potentiality, Vj(p) a sufficient pathway, and ci its Conditions of Power.

### P(S,T) = {p1, p2, …, pn}.

**Power(p,T) = positive** if at least one pathway Vj(p) is fully satisfied at T; **negative** otherwise. Documentary uncertainty is reported separately. Sufficiency concerns the ability to **undertake** Actualization, not the success of the result.

**[FORMALIZATION]** Illustrative index of condition satisfaction for a pathway: **I = 100 × Σ(wi × si) / Σ(wi)**, where wi ≥ 0 and si ranges from 0 to 1, provided Σ(wi) > 0. This index does not replace binary Power and is not a probability. Pathway comparison, calibration, and dependencies between conditions remain **[OPEN]**.

# 9. Modes of use and applications
**[VALIDATED]** Provide a guided **simple mode**, with relevant criteria and intelligible justifications, and an **advanced mode** allowing adjustment of scenarios, weights, dependencies, uncertainties, and indirect effects without bypassing fundamental principles.

**Applications to explore [POTENTIAL; effectiveness not demonstrated]**: analysis of behaviors and social dynamics; conflict clarification; individual support; institutions and public policy; organizational and technical design; philosophical and semantic clarification; artificial intelligence systems capable of adaptive trialectic dialogue. The Method's comparative value remains to be tested.

A comparison performed by a single model is not independent validation.
